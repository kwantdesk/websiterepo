# SOCIAL MEDIA

## Kwantdesk competitor analysis and 90-day X growth plan

**Research date:** 22 August 2026  
**Accounts reviewed:** [Varun Takkar](https://x.com/varuntakkar01), [Simply0DTE](https://x.com/Simply0DTE), [Glitch](https://x.com/Glitch_Trades), and [Garma](https://x.com/garma_don_)  
**Method:** Live review of each public X profile, its positioning, pinned post, and a small sample of visible recent posts. Counts are snapshots and will change. This is a strategic content review, not a complete historical export or an audit of trading performance.

---

## Executive conclusion

Kwantdesk is not competing with four isolated trading creators. It is competing with a **creator-led distribution system**.

Glitch, Simply0DTE, and Garma reinforce the same Skylit story from different positions:

- **Glitch** supplies founder authority, spectacle, reach, and a strong identity.
- **Simply0DTE** supplies high-volume product education, community wins, testimonials, and conversion offers.
- **Garma** supplies niche practitioner proof: exact Nasdaq levels, live-session reactions, and beginner-relatable usage.
- **Varun Takkar**, while separate from that group, demonstrates the adjacent monetised-trader playbook: large outcome claims, technical detail, exact contracts, visual evidence, and a paid-product funnel.

Their popularity comes from a repeatable combination: a narrow identity, confident hooks, visible proof, specific market information, prediction callbacks, a recognisable personality, and relentless repetition. The Skylit group then multiplies that effect by letting several accounts tell the product story in different voices.

Kwantdesk should copy the **distribution mechanics**, not the casino-like framing. Its strongest opening is to become the home of **timestamped reasoning and accountable review**:

> Other trading accounts show the result. Kwantdesk shows what the trader knew, what they planned, what changed, and what the evidence says afterward.

That distinction is credible, difficult to fake, and consistent with the existing Socials product vision: **Plan it. Prove it. Review it. Repeat.**

## Account-by-account analysis

## 1. Varun Takkar — aspirational proof plus technical specificity

### Positioning

Varun's profile bio is deliberately modest—“Learner.”—while his content projects strong conviction and large financial outcomes. That tension makes the account feel approachable and aspirational at once. The paid link in the profile turns attention into a commercial funnel.

### What makes the account popular

1. **The proof is emotionally large.** The [pinned post](https://x.com/varuntakkar01/status/2060369130721624078) says he made $100K in one month and compares it with an entire annual 9-to-5 salary. It received about 57K views, 105 likes, and 23 replies in the observed snapshot.
2. **Technical posts contain valuable specifics.** An [Intel heatmap post](https://x.com/varuntakkar01/status/2090922922127012232) names the spot level, the $97.50 strike, and more than $211M in premium. It had roughly 17K views and 158 likes.
3. **He creates continuing stories rather than isolated calls.** A separate [Intel technical analysis](https://x.com/varuntakkar01/status/2090844014870179902) describes an inverse head-and-shoulders pattern and $90 support. Later posts can return to that thesis.
4. **He uses before/after callbacks.** An [IBIT recap](https://x.com/varuntakkar01/status/2090882194315329980) quotes the earlier trade alert, includes the exact contract and intended entry, then reports trimming at 70% gains. A [Google callback](https://x.com/varuntakkar01/status/2090847800334397512) asks, “Did the trampoline work?” while resurfacing the earlier idea.
5. **Visual evidence does much of the persuasion.** Heatmaps, charts, and quoted earlier posts let readers verify part of the story without reading a long explanation.

### Weaknesses and openings for Kwantdesk

- Large outcome claims attract attention but can look hindsight-led when the complete decision trail is not visible.
- Hyperbolic wording and a sales link may create scepticism among serious traders.
- Outcome-led proof does not necessarily show whether the underlying process was repeatable.

### Lesson for Kwantdesk

Use Varun's specificity—levels, invalidation, timing, evidence, and callbacks—but attach it to an immutable pre-trade record. The Kwantdesk version of this content should make the chronology impossible to misunderstand.

## 2. Simply0DTE — product evangelism and community amplification

### Positioning

Simply0DTE presents as an experienced options trader, a heatmap specialist, and an active member of the Skylit ecosystem. The account combines trader identity, product support, humour, member testimonials, and direct sales offers.

### What makes the account popular

1. **The product is always connected to a trading situation.** A [paper-trading and charting update](https://x.com/Simply0DTE/status/2090839540046897538) is framed around using the feature for a live CRCL squeeze rather than listing software functions in isolation.
2. **Community wins become social proof.** [“This is the new norm”](https://x.com/Simply0DTE/status/2090896135061688600) highlights a Discord member result. Another [post credits collaborators and the Skylit Discord](https://x.com/Simply0DTE/status/2090866988356669576) for identifying a Tesla squeeze.
3. **He makes other people visible.** Crediting members and partner accounts encourages them to reply, repost, and identify with the product.
4. **Personality prevents the feed from becoming a software manual.** A [humorous Korea post](https://x.com/Simply0DTE/status/2090614415280222385) about showing every trader Skylit and “saving” MU and DRAM earned about 14K views and 149 likes—stronger visible like performance than several functional product posts.
5. **Offers are explicit.** The [pinned post](https://x.com/Simply0DTE/status/2057842734665269335) combines a rollout, tiered products, discount codes, and scarcity. It reached about 150K views.
6. **Volume builds familiarity.** More than 7,000 observed posts in just over two years means the audience repeatedly encounters the same product-category association.

### Weaknesses and openings for Kwantdesk

- The feed can feel highly promotional.
- Discounting and scarcity can train the audience to wait for offers.
- Member screenshots are persuasive but less trustworthy than independently timestamped evidence.
- Product enthusiasm sometimes outruns explanation of the decision process.

### Lesson for Kwantdesk

Turn customers into visible teachers, not anonymous testimonial screenshots. Every community feature should point to a verifiable Precord, review, or evidence chain. Let the user earn attention and reputation while the product receives secondary credit.

## 3. Glitch — founder authority, spectacle, and a memorable identity

### Positioning

Glitch has the clearest identity of the group: “The Glitch on Wall Street.” The name, confidence, founder status, and content style all reinforce a single character. The account behaves like a media personality first and a company founder second.

### What makes the account popular

1. **The identity is instantly repeatable.** People can describe the character in one sentence. That is essential for word-of-mouth.
2. **Hooks create tension.** The [pinned post](https://x.com/Glitch_Trades/status/2014350711752921249) opens with “Am I the best $SPX trader on X?” and follows with zero drawdown and a $150K day. The visible snapshot showed about 946K views, 1.6K likes, 179 replies, and 77 reposts.
3. **Proof is packaged as entertainment.** Video, P&L, provocation, and conflict create a clip people can share even if they do not know the product.
4. **The account repeatedly closes loops.** A [Tesla callback](https://x.com/Glitch_Trades/status/2090965028069372111) resurfaces an earlier long-term call with “This aging well.”
5. **Free value strengthens goodwill.** A [weekly sector-watchlist post](https://x.com/Glitch_Trades/status/2090955775866773708) calls the newsletter a gift before time away. It received about 23K views and 390 likes.
6. **Small human posts support the large claims.** GIFs, weekend greetings, affection for the audience, and brief opinions make the profile feel like a person rather than a campaign.
7. **Longevity compounds authority.** The account dates to 2020 and showed more than 14,000 posts, giving it a large archive and many chances to be discovered.

### Weaknesses and openings for Kwantdesk

- “Best trader” framing and raw P&L can feel combative or casino-like.
- Spectacular wins can dominate attention while risk and ordinary sessions disappear.
- A founder-personality brand can become dependent on one person.

### Lesson for Kwantdesk

Kwantdesk needs a recognisable founder/operator voice with real opinions. It should borrow Glitch's clarity, confidence, brevity, proof packaging, and community warmth—without copying unverifiable superlatives or making raw P&L the reputation system.

## 4. Garma — niche practitioner proof and live market utility

### Positioning

Garma is much smaller and newer than the other accounts, but has a sharp niche: full-time NQ trading using Skylit plus order flow. The voice is raw, brief, and session-oriented.

### What makes the account popular

1. **The niche is narrow.** Followers know they will see Nasdaq futures, exact levels, and the Skylit workflow.
2. **Posts are timely and useful.** A [key-level post](https://x.com/garma_don_/status/2090429335790309625) lists 29556, 29434, 29270, and 29143. A later [$NQ 270 callback](https://x.com/garma_don_/status/2090469903870341158) returns to that map.
3. **Short callbacks manufacture proof.** A [“saw the dump coming” post](https://x.com/garma_don_/status/2090806540143931883) quotes the [earlier map check](https://x.com/garma_don_/status/2090789238425960539), letting the reader see the sequence.
4. **The voice feels native to the trading session.** Phrases such as “270 bang?” and “let's see how the maps look” feel like messages from a trading desk rather than polished brand copy.
5. **The funnel goes beyond X.** The [pinned post](https://x.com/garma_don_/status/2086675782357717503) directs educational recaps to YouTube and trades to Instagram. At roughly 11K views against 1,128 followers, it achieved nearly 10× follower reach.
6. **The audience can grow with the creator.** Eight months of stated experience creates a more accessible journey than a finished-guru identity.

### Weaknesses and openings for Kwantdesk

- The authority base is early and currently tied closely to another product's identity.
- Very short recaps can look like hindsight unless timestamps and complete plans remain visible.
- The content offers exact levels but limited structured reflection on invalidation, mistakes, and process quality.

### Lesson for Kwantdesk

Recruit credible niche practitioners before they are huge. Give them a repeatable content object—a Kwantdesk market map or Precord—that is useful before the move and naturally quotable afterward.

## The real competitor: the Skylit creator system

The three Skylit-aligned accounts cover different parts of the funnel:

| Funnel job | Account | Content used | Audience effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attract attention | Glitch | Spectacular proof, bold opinions, founder identity, free newsletter | “This person and product are important.” |
| Explain and convert | Simply0DTE | Product demonstrations, offers, community wins, humour | “I understand what the product does and others use it.” |
| Make it relatable | Garma | Exact NQ levels, live reactions, tutorials and recaps | “A trader like me can use this in a real session.” |
| Reinforce socially | All three | Tags, credits, quote posts, shared product language | “This is a movement, not a single advertisement.” |

This is stronger than relying on a company account. Product claims are repeated by different people, in different formats, at different levels of fame.

### Implication

Kwantdesk should build a small **creator bench**:

- a founder/operator voice for category opinions and vision;
- the official Kwantdesk account for product truth, evidence, and launches;
- two to five practitioner accounts in specific niches such as SPX/0DTE, NQ, swing options, and futures;
- customer educators whose successful content earns distribution from the official account.

The people should not paste identical copy. Each needs a clear role and natural voice while using the same underlying proof system.

## What Kwantdesk should copy—and what it should reject

### Copy these mechanics

- **A one-line identity:** every account must be describable in one sentence.
- **Specificity:** name the instrument, level, scenario, invalidation, timeframe, and evidence.
- **Prediction callbacks:** quote the timestamped original and explain what happened.
- **Visual proof:** charts, heatmaps, plan cards, evidence changes, and review cards.
- **Founder personality:** opinions, humour, frustration, lessons, and visible conviction.
- **Community amplification:** credit the trader, analyst, or customer who did the work.
- **Product-in-workflow content:** demonstrate the job being done, not a list of features.
- **Free recurring value:** a weekly map, report, review, or teardown that readers anticipate.
- **Cross-platform depth:** X for discovery, short video for explanation, and a durable newsletter or site page for retention.
- **Consistent repetition:** own a small number of ideas until the market associates them with Kwantdesk.

### Reject these patterns

- Raw P&L as the main reputation score.
- “Best trader” claims that cannot be independently established.
- Deleted or edited losing calls.
- Screenshots without the original timestamped plan.
- Guaranteed-outcome language or copy-trading implications.
- Artificial scarcity as the default sales tactic.
- Engagement bait disconnected from product value.
- A generic feed that talks about every market but owns no category.

## Kwantdesk's own category position

### Recommended position

**Kwantdesk is the proof layer for serious traders.**

Supporting line:

> Plan the trade before it happens. Capture the evidence. Review the decision honestly.

### Why this is defensible

Competitors can post another winning screenshot. It is harder to reproduce a system that records:

1. the thesis and evidence before the outcome;
2. the exact timestamp and immutable history;
3. the invalidation and scenario map;
4. execution receipts where available;
5. an honest post-trade review;
6. a reputation based on process quality over time.

The content itself becomes a product demonstration. Every strong public Precord can acquire a user; every review can retain one; every credible profile can recruit another creator.

### Message hierarchy

1. **Category:** trading decisions with receipts.
2. **Core promise:** prove your process, not merely your outcome.
3. **Product object:** the timestamped Precord.
4. **Emotional benefit:** confidence that your story is honest and your process is improving.
5. **Social benefit:** earn reputation from preparation, evidence, and review—not follower count.

## 90-day content strategy

## Content mix

| Share | Pillar | Purpose | Typical format |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 35% | Timestamped market maps | Deliver useful pre-market information and establish credibility before outcomes | Precord card, chart, scenarios, invalidation |
| 20% | Callbacks and reviews | Close the loop visibly, including misses | Quote post plus outcome and lesson |
| 15% | Product in the workflow | Show how Kwantdesk supports a real decision | Short screen recording or annotated card |
| 15% | Education | Teach evidence, review, risk, and decision quality | Thread, carousel, short video |
| 10% | Founder/operator point of view | Build identity and category leadership | Strong opinion, story, reply, humour |
| 5% | Community proof | Create advocates and demonstrate varied use cases | User spotlight linked to a public Precord |

Winning posts can be adapted across pillars, but the feed should never become 80% release notes or testimonials.

## Weekly cadence

### Official Kwantdesk account

- **Monday:** weekly market map or “three scenarios we are watching.”
- **Tuesday:** 30–60 second workflow demonstration tied to a real decision.
- **Wednesday:** evidence-based educational thread or teardown.
- **Thursday:** community Precord spotlight with the creator credited.
- **Friday:** weekly proof report—what worked, what failed, what changed.
- **Daily:** thoughtful replies under target creators and traders; one to three concise posts only when there is genuine value.

### Founder account

- Three high-conviction category opinions per week.
- Two build-in-public or product-learning posts per week.
- One personal story, mistake, or humorous observation per week.
- Active replies to practitioners, with no canned product pitch.

### Practitioner accounts

- Publish plans before sessions.
- Quote the original plan when a scenario activates or fails.
- Record one structured weekly review.
- Explain where Kwantdesk helped organise or preserve evidence; never claim the tool caused the trade to win.

## A repeatable five-part post structure

1. **Hook:** a sharp observation, conflict, or useful promise.
2. **Specifics:** ticker, timeframe, level, scenario, evidence.
3. **Invalidation:** what would make the idea wrong.
4. **Proof:** link or image of the timestamped Precord.
5. **Callback:** return later whether the idea won, lost, or remained unresolved.

This structure combines the competitors' strongest distribution mechanics with Kwantdesk's trust advantage.

## Creator and ambassador plan

### Stage 1: Founding practitioners, days 1–30

Recruit three to five traders with small but credible audiences. Prefer clear specialists over generic “finance creators.” Assess them on consistency, willingness to show invalidations, clarity of explanation, and audience trust—not follower count.

Give each practitioner:

- a defined niche;
- a public profile and Precord format;
- a weekly review slot on the official account;
- lightweight help turning one plan into a post, short video, and weekly lesson;
- transparent product access, disclosed where relevant.

### Stage 2: Evidence-led amplification, days 31–60

The official account reposts the best work based on process quality. Introduce a weekly creator round-up and one collaborative live review. Record which practitioner brings profile visits, qualified replies, sign-ups, and returning users.

### Stage 3: Repeatable creator programme, days 61–90

Invite the strongest contributors into a named programme. Create standards for disclosure, proof, conduct, and attribution. Reward durable education and verified product feedback rather than loud profit claims.

The programme should feel like a professional trading desk with different specialists—not an affiliate army posting identical advertisements.

## Measurement framework

Follower count is useful context but should not become the goal. Track the content-to-product path.

### Distribution metrics

- Median views per post, not only the largest winner.
- Views divided by followers for cross-account comparison.
- Repost and bookmark rate for useful content.
- Qualified replies from actual traders.
- Profile visits and new relevant followers.

### Trust metrics

- Percentage of market calls published before the outcome.
- Percentage later closed with a review.
- Percentage of failed or invalidated ideas still publicly visible.
- Number of public records referenced by third parties.
- Repeat engagement from the same credible practitioners.

### Product metrics

- X profile → site click-through rate.
- Visitor → account creation rate.
- Account creation → first Precord rate.
- First Precord → completed review rate.
- Four-week creator and user retention.
- Invited users and activated users per practitioner.

### Recommended 90-day targets

Set baselines in the first two weeks, then target:

- 2× median views per post;
- at least 80% of public market plans closed with a review;
- 12 strong public before/after loops each month;
- three active founding practitioners by day 30 and five by day 90;
- a measurable improvement each month in profile-to-first-Precord conversion.

Avoid arbitrary follower targets until baseline conversion and retention are known.

## Guardrails

Kwantdesk's content should remain aligned with its product principles:

- timestamp all evidence-sensitive claims;
- keep losing and invalidated records visible;
- distinguish plans, observations, executions, and outcomes;
- do not imply guaranteed returns, personalised financial advice, or copy trading;
- disclose paid relationships, free access, or ambassador arrangements;
- obtain permission before featuring a customer's record;
- never rank people solely by raw P&L, follower count, or engagement;
- reward reasoning quality, evidence discipline, honest review, and consistency;
- do not claim that Kwantdesk caused a profitable outcome merely because it recorded the process.

The aim is not to sound timid. It is to make bold claims that the evidence can support.

## Bottom line

Glitch and the surrounding Skylit accounts win because they combine **personality, proof, specificity, repetition, and a multi-creator network**. Kwantdesk will not beat that system with polished company announcements alone.

It can build something more durable: a network of credible practitioners whose plans are useful before the market moves and whose reviews remain honest afterward. If every public post reinforces that standard, the product and the media strategy become the same thing.

**Their strongest message is:** “Look what we caught.”  
**Kwantdesk's stronger long-term message can be:** “Here is what we knew, when we knew it, how we acted, and what we learned.”
